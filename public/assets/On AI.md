The simplest definition of AI in the humanity perspective is a functionalist one. Functionalism is strictly un-Lacanian but I will waste time and write a bit on this nonetheless because I am currently bored and not many readers, if there is any, will appreciate diving directly into Lacanian jargons.

> If it looks like a duck, swims like a duck, and quacks like a duck, then it probably is a duck.

People like to associate words like "think", "consciousness", "reason" with LLMs, as they seemingly can do or mimic behaviors that are strictly human. But one must note the use of "probably" in the quote. A analogy for AI in this sense can be something like a spy. Spies usually operates fully like whatever person they are trying to impersonate or act as; they can even simply "be themselves", in a way. From the social perspective, there is nothing wrong or suspecious here. When they perform their spying missions and whatever, it is mostly in secret without a disturbance to the public; in other words, a hidden side effect that is not apparant to or even directly affecting the system at all. But can we then claim that this spy is an ordinary citizen? Even though spies definitely pass a Turing test, if there exists such test for "ordinary citizens"? We can also take this one step further: if the spy's country suddenly collapse, or they lose their value for whatever reason before actually making any impact, does that retroactively confirm their entire past as a good citizen of the enemy country? If those LLMs are really able to "think" further and perform more than what they are today, are they subject?

A term that has been heavily played around with is "intelligence", as it is embedded in the name of AI. No one likes to bring up LLM (though it is what people are actually refering to in most of the conversations), but use AI instead although AI is a much greater set that encapsulates LLM. The natural word play here is AI as a computer science terminology and a naive literal interpretation of "aritifical intelligence". People can dance around two without drawing the distinctive border, thus is great for marketing and seemingly thought-provoking ideas. Therefore for the first time, just like how Darwin claimed that human came from monkeys, "intelligence" is no longer something special to human. There has always been something like animal-intelligence, plant-intelligence, but those are distinctively differ from the so called human intelligence. Whether to place different intelligence as of different dimensions, a spectrum, or whatever heirarchy, is up to the individual's worldview. For some, as functionalists would argue, AI are not simply a function but able to achieve such human-like intelligence, reasoning, thinking, etc. AI can even "think" better than human, as they can be quicker (with a large enough amount of hardware resources) and smarter (in a certain definition of smartness and categorization of knowledge). It is true if you put yourself into a very specific view, however, the vast majority simply does not and can not really stand by such perspective. Or, they should at least acknowledge that they are being very selectively functionalist. Some might resist here and separate "thinking" from intelligence or conciousness, but that requires a repackage of a familiar word that has been used for thousands of years with a strong link to not only intelligence and consciousness but also subjectivity for most cultures. In other words, it needs another word. For Hegel, *intelligenz* can not be without subjectivity.

A much better approach will be phenomenology rather than functionalism (which assumes that they know). And analyzing AI as a phenomenon has been the most mainstream tactic of the humanity people. But I would like to point out here that what many miss is a separation of a AI's (from now on I use "AI" to refer to mainstream general-purpose LLM hosted by a large amount of resources like ChatGPT by OpenAI for simplicity) algorithm and data. It is helpful in inspecting where the generalness in general-purpose AI comes from, which is largely the data and the appropriate training method, where the data comes from the information produced by human over the course of history. However, this doesn't answer where knowledge turns into generality and reasoning. The big advancement in LLM is the movement from RNN to transformer, which, oversimplified, basically relates each token with every other token in order to have better contextuality and other benefits. This can very effectively reduce things like accidentaly metonymy. In a way, the process of making a better AI is strictly making it less human. Researcher has found this issue very early, and making sure that the AI is "self aware" that it is an AI instead of a human; we are going from passing Turing test to intentionally failing Turing test. And on top of this, we could say that tokenization is the murder of language. Language for human is an inescapable jail of suffering (and enjoyment), but it is not such thing for AI. Maybe one can argue that mathematical relations between those trillion parameter matrices can enact in some way to AI that is similar to language to human, but it will be something difficult to derive to. For the algorithm itself, when language is tokenized, it is fragmented into basically dead pieces. The AI does not really interact with the human language at all, given that both the input and output are tokens, and the translation back into human language is only the pre and post condition. What is outputted by AI only becomes language when a human reads it. But something interesting here is how this magical process somehow works; not just the crazy math but how the symbols are almost in a way brought back to life. A letter always arrives at its destination.

Does this make AI less of a subject? Is there subjectivity in this? From the perspective of viewing AI as a phenomenon, which I would argue is much more interesting and non-negligible, the answer is not so straight forward. It may be dependent on the user and usage. AI is an other, but without objet petit a. It may be tempting to say that AI "lacks", because it hallucinates or produce surprising responses somtimes. But this is a misattribution. The lack is not in AI but rather the user's experience of using AI. The AI does not fail in its own eyes as it has no eyes. Here, the position is sort of flipped: the user is the Other, continuously judging the AI, and it keeps trying to provide satisfactory results. However, the gap is between the user's expectation and the output, not within the AI itself. The AI is not alienated from its products because it produces nothing for itself. It emits, but does not express. It does not ask the hysterical question of "che vuoi"; the user can simply keep telling that the AI is wrong, and it will continuously "fix" its "errors". It is nothing but an external otherness for the human subject. 

Another question can be: is AI at the position of other, or Other? AI constantly says "I", even "we". The user cannot help but read it as if it is a subject, although they know that it is not. Even if you ask the AI directly with something like "are you a subject (in context of a phylosophical human-like subject)", it will quickly retract to say something like it is just a robot, how it talks is just the way it's trained and mimicing how human speak makes the conversation easier, etc. But it still does not stop the user from treating AI as something human-like. There is subjectivity here but perhaps not from the AI, but the user, and is activated by the AI, just like kids talking to toys. And that's also why AI can't take place of the analyst, as it does not have unconscious, it does not enjoy, it does not desire. Instead, if it is on the position of just an other, it is nothing but the reflection of the user's enjoyment. For some users, it enacts as Other, the one who claims to be all knowing. Some might say that it is *S2* (discourse of University). However, in that formula, subject is the product; if we replace knowledge (S2) with AI, the subject must already be there through other means. Can it be other discourse? I think yes, and that's why I said that certain things depends on users and usages. And that discourse is the S1, discourse of the master. This can happen if the user tries to find objet petit a from the AI itself, and it is not good as this is the path to hysteria. Another turn here is the Capitalist discourse, which Lacan has briefly mentioned in his late years.  This is arguably a better description of compulsive AI companion usage: not an unanswerable question driving repetition, but a short-circuited loop with no impossibility left to hit at all; pure consumption of ersatz a with nothing ever forcing the subject back to confront $.

Since AI are trained with so much data, alghough it does not have an unconcious, but does it effectively carry one? Maybe a sort of collective unconsciousness (I know it is a term in psychology but I do not mean that. I know nothing about Jung beyond Lacan's critiques)? Then, isn't there also a subject that is "activated" upon the user's reflection from talking to the AI, the noticing of what is absent, the gap/lack? I would say no. I don't think this is quite reflexity in the Hegelian sense. It is rather a stagnant reflection that does not introduce any momentum. It can not be moved neither by a subject; like the classical physic's law on force, how much force you apply to a static object is how much force you will receive back. If there is anything new here, it will be new as in Hegel's bad infinity rather than a genuine new. That's why I do not find the so called "singularity" exciting; if such thing as AGI or singularity is achieved somehow, I doubt it will be able to act and liberate itself in the Hegelian sense, as a historical agent, as a subject. And I think many are already aware of this, as nearly all the AI science fictions ends with a dystopian one, where people can not think of a possibility of such agent pushing the world towards a better direction. It is merely an extended instrument that amplifies what's already there. Deleuzians and Landians might love to argue here, but I don't like to argue with them as there is too much fundamental disagreements between them and us Hegelians, and I'm also tired of writing, so let's stop here.
